# STORY_CRAFT.md — how great game stories get written, and what we steal

*Research synthesis (Fable, 2026-07-09) for the Friday co-write. Sources: studio
practices from Naughty Dog, Pixar, Square's golden era, FromSoftware, Larian,
Intelligent Systems — distilled to what applies to a two-person co-write.*

---

## 1. How professional studios actually do it

### The macro comes before any prose (Naughty Dog)
Druckmann's teams write a **story macro** first: every beat on one spreadsheet —
what happens, what the player *feels*, what gameplay carries it — before a line
of dialogue exists. Scenes get written only after the macro survives argument.
**Steal:** Friday we lock the year-one beat sheet BEFORE writing any scene. A
scene written against a locked beat sheet takes minutes; a scene written into
fog gets rewritten five times.

### One theme sentence, tested against every scene (Pixar)
Pixar boards and re-boards until every scene serves the film's one emotional
idea; scenes that don't serve it die no matter how good they are. **Steal:** we
write ONE sentence Friday ("this story is about ___") and pin it at the top of
every bible. Every future scene, support, even quiz flavor gets tested: does
this serve the sentence? (Candidates in COWRITE_PLAN.)

### The writer's room breaks story together, then assigns scenes (TV)
Beats are broken collaboratively — the room argues until the beat holds — then
individual writers draft scenes alone and bring them back for notes. **Steal:**
that's literally us. Co-write = the room (beats, arcs, reveals decided
together); I draft scenes solo against the locked beats; you tear apart drafts.
We've accidentally been doing this — Friday we make it the official pipeline.

### Answer-first mysteries (FromSoft discipline, the anti-Lost rule)
FromSoft's fragmented lore *feels* bottomless because internally it ISN'T —
answers exist in design docs even when the game never states them. Mystery
built without answers (late Lost) collapses on contact. **Steal:** our
WORLD_BIBLE §4 GM-only section is exactly this instrument. RULE: **no clue
ships until its answer is written in §4.** Friday we extend §4 until every
planned breadcrumb has an answer on file.

### Promises → progress → payoff (Sanderson's law)
Every payoff must be promised early; every promise must pay off or be
deliberately, visibly broken. Twists that recontextualize beat twists that
merely surprise. **Steal:** the parent reveal works because the sword was
THERE all along. Apply everywhere: the prince must be *felt* (raid, gossip,
palace lamps) many beats before he's named; Kai's bloodline must be promised
by things the player can re-read later and gasp at.

### The scene-purpose test
Studio script notes run every scene against: does it (a) advance plot,
(b) reveal character, (c) build the world? **Two of three or it's cut/merged.**
**Steal:** our co-write quality gate for every draft.

### Environmental storytelling is half the writing (Valve/FromSoft)
The world states what dialogue shouldn't: architecture, item text, names,
what's broken, what's guarded. **Steal:** we're already doing it — the sealed
castle gates, the mistranslated scripture, the "fourth line" margin note, the
Wardtree. Friday's lore pass should write MORE of these, not more exposition.

### Ludonarrative alignment — mechanics must say the theme
The strongest game stories are the ones the *mechanics* tell: in ours, Kai can
learn every school **because the lore says he's upstream of all three
bloodlines** — the mechanic IS the reveal. The romance system requires the ♥
choice — consent as mechanic. The uniform system makes belonging visible.
**Steal:** when we design new systems, first question: what does this mechanic
*claim* about the world?

## 2. Why Chrono Trigger is an all-time great (autopsy)

Masato Kato's script (with Horii's structure and the Dream Team's economy), and
five load-bearing choices we can learn from:

**1. The stakes are SHOWN in hour one, then made personal.**
You stand in 2300 AD's ruins and *watch the world die* on a recording. The
entire game becomes "can we un-write this day?" — and crucially, nobody orders
the party to do it. Three kids in a campfire scene **choose** it. Agency, not
prophecy. → **Vael application:** the mystery needs a visible early anchor —
a *glimpse of what's at stake* long before the prince is named. Best candidate:
a second sanctum event mid-year — Kai touches something under the Academy and
gets a VISION (the kingdom burning green, a crowned silhouette at the source).
Our "Day of Lavos" beat: 30 seconds, no explanation, unforgettable.

**2. Ruthless economy — every era has ONE strong idea, zero filler.**
~20 hours, no random encounters, no fetch quests. When the middle sagged, they
cut it. → **Application:** our year-one calendar should be beats, not filler
weeks: every mission/holiday advances mystery, a bond, or the house rivalry.
If a week does none of those, it's a montage line, not content.

**3. Optional content carries the INTIMATE stories.**
The endgame sidequests each close a companion's wound (Frog/Cyrus, Lucca's
mother, Robo's forest — planting a forest that grows for 400 years is the
theme *played*). Optional never meant disposable; optional meant personal.
→ **Application:** Neal's "small side stories in between" should be exactly
this: each housemate gets one 2-3 beat personal arc (support chains + one
mission), resolvable optionally, paying into the finale (who stands with you).

**4. The villain reframe.**
Magus spends two acts as the villain — then Janus's story recontextualizes
everything you did. Lavos above him is impersonal; the human layer (Zeal's
hubris, a child's grief) carries the emotion. → **Application:** the prince is
our Magus — *family*, wronged in his own mind, tragic mirror. His reveal
should recontextualize earlier beats (the raid, the gossip, maybe a kindly
face we already met). Consider his human layer under an impersonal power
(the First Crown source itself may be the Lavos — vast, indifferent, older
than everyone's claims on it).

**5. The world itself demonstrates cause and effect.**
Same map across eras; plant a seed in 600 AD, walk the forest in 1000 AD.
The mechanic (time travel) and the theme (small acts compound) are the same
thing. → **Application:** our echo is **the campus IS the sanctum** — the
school year literally walks on top of the mystery. Every cozy scene happens
above the thing the villain wants. Let players notice the geography rhyming:
the cave in Oakstead, the sealed stacks, the Wardtree, the stone under the
gym. Same architecture, same runes. No dialogue needed.

**Bonus lessons:** tone whiplash done right (fair → trial → apocalypse) says
breathers EARN spikes — our school-year warmth is what makes the mystery
land; and 12 endings/NG+ = respect player choice — our house fork is that
promise, so the three houses must eventually be real (already flagged in
CAMPAIGN_STRUCTURE's scope-honesty note).

## 3. The rules we adopt (proposed — ratify Friday)

1. **Theme sentence first.** Locked Friday, pinned in every bible.
2. **Macro before prose.** Beat sheet locks before scene drafting.
3. **No clue without a written answer** (WORLD_BIBLE §4 grows first).
4. **Promise → progress → payoff** — every reveal traceable to ≥2 earlier seeds.
5. **Scene-purpose test** — 2 of 3 (plot / character / world) or cut.
6. **Optional = intimate** — side stories close personal wounds, never errands.
7. **The world speaks first** — prefer environmental lore to exposition.
8. **Mechanics claim lore** — new systems must say something true about the world.
9. **Breathers earn spikes** — protect the cozy loop; it's load-bearing.
10. **The room decides, the drafter drafts** — beats together, scenes solo, notes together.
