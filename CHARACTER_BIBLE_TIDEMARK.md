# CHARACTER_BIBLE_TIDEMARK.md — House Tidemark

> **THEME (locked 2026-07-09):** *Belonging isn't given by blood — it's built by what you protect.*


*Deep blue & silver · Leviathan · the coast · lances / armor / faith · defense growths (DEF/HP)*
*Culture: disciplined, chivalric, duty-bound. Hold the line so others don't fall.*

Companion to the other bibles. All-new roster (Tidemark had only Serra + generic students in the build). Serra (heir) is in `CHARACTER_BIBLE_HEADS.md`. Roster coverage: anchor (Osric), healer (Elowen), magic (Isolde), mobility/outlier (Fenn), and a second frontliner with reach (Bertram) — full spread, all in the house's defensive/faith idiom, with Fenn built in as the pressure-release valve so the house isn't five shades of solemn.

---

## Osric — Armored Lancer / Great Knight track
*lance · heavy armor · immovable · DEF/HP anchor*

- **Role:** the wall of walls — even tankier than Emberwing's Brenna, the house's defensive philosophy made flesh.
- **Personality:** by-the-book to a fault. Rules are the thin line between order and the chaos his coastal home has drowned under before. Formal, earnest, deeply decent — and genuinely rattled by anything that doesn't fit procedure, which is Kai's entire existence.
- **Depth:** his rigidity is fear wearing a uniform — he's seen what happens when the line breaks, and he'd rather be laughed at for over-preparing than be right too late. Warms into the most reliable friend in the game once he decides you're inside the wall he's guarding.
- **With Kai:** starts stiff and suspicious, becomes staunchly loyal — the arc of a rule-follower learning that Kai's rule-breaking comes from the same place his rule-keeping does: protect the vulnerable. Romance-optional (earnest, formal, endearingly out of his depth).

## Elowen — Faith Healer / Priest
*staff · faith magic · the house's dedicated healer*

- **Role:** Tidemark's healer, and the most devout unit in the game — faith as literal power.
- **Personality:** serene, warm, unshakably grounded. Believes duty and even suffering carry meaning, and that belief makes her a genuine anchor for a frightened, uprooted Kai — she's the one person who treats his awakening as *sacred* rather than dangerous or fascinating.
- **Depth:** her serenity is tested, not naive. The campaign's mystery — magic reaching where it "shouldn't," the wards failing — strikes at the heart of everything her faith is built on, and watching her hold or reforge that faith is one of Tidemark's best long arcs.
- **With Kai:** gentle, spiritually intimate; she gives him the first framework where his magic isn't a curse. Romance candidate (the devoted, soulful one).

## Isolde — Reason Mage
*tome · magic · precise, doctrinaire*

- **Role:** Tidemark's ranged magic — the tome user the current chapter already implies, now a person.
- **Personality:** exacting, proud, correct — and she knows it. Casts strictly from approved doctrine, distrusts improvisation, and Kai's untrained, rules-free magic offends her on a professional level before it intrigues her on a human one.
- **Depth:** the rigidity masks real insecurity — she earned her precision, wasn't gifted ease, and Kai's raw natural talent is everything she resents and secretly envies. Her arc bends toward respect for the thing she can't quite categorize.
- **With Kai:** friction-to-fascination; two casters from opposite ends of "how magic should work." Romance-optional (the sharp one who softens).

## Fenn — Archer *(the house outlier)*
*bow · mobile · breaks the lance/armor mold on purpose*

- **Role:** Tidemark's mobility and range — deliberately off-identity, which is the point.
- **Personality:** sardonic, quick, quietly rebellious. Chafes against Tidemark's rigidity constantly — the student who loves the *house* and can't stand the *rules* — and stays loyal anyway, which makes the friction affectionate rather than bitter. The comic pressure-valve so the house isn't monotone solemnity.
- **Depth:** the sarcasm hides someone who bought into Tidemark's ideals harder than anyone and got burned by the gap between the ideals and the institution. Wants the house to be *better*, not gone. Kai — an outsider who sees the house clearly — is someone Fenn can finally be honest with.
- **With Kai:** instant easy rapport, two people allergic to pretense. Romance candidate (the witty, guard-up one who lets Kai past it).

## Bertram — Soldier / Halberdier *(new — the house's fifth)*
*lance · sturdy midline · reach and reliability*

- **Role:** the dependable middle — less immovable than Osric, more mobile, the house's workhorse spear.
- **Personality:** older-feeling, steady, wry — from a small, service-minded lesser house that's *fine* with the rules because those rules gave a family with no great name a ladder to climb. Uncomplaining, quietly wise, the one who's seen enough to not panic.
- **Depth:** a foil for both Osric (rules as fear vs. rules as gratitude) and Fenn (rules as cage vs. rules as ladder) — same house, three totally different relationships to the same discipline. With Kai, he's the calm veteran voice.
- **With Kai:** mentor-ish camaraderie, low drama, high warmth. Romance-optional (default platonic).

## Teacher — Maren, the Tidewarden
*head of house · discipline embodied, doctrine privately doubted*

- **Personality:** the picture of Tidemark — immaculate, controlled, principled. And privately, quietly, coming apart at a seam nobody sees: she watched doctrine *fail* once, watched the rulebook get good people killed by being followed too well, and she has never resolved it. Teaches the discipline flawlessly while no longer fully believing it's enough.
- **The stake:** she is the adult version of Serra's exact crisis (rulebook vs. right thing), which makes her the mirror the campaign can hold up to the whole house. Her private doubt connects to the mystery — the failure she witnessed may have been an early wardstone incident dressed up as a tactical loss, and she's spent years not letting herself ask.
- **With Kai:** wary, then reluctantly moved. Kai is everything her doctrine has no box for, and watching him do right without a rulebook is a slow, uncomfortable education for the teacher, not just the student.
