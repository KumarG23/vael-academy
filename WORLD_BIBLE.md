# WORLD_BIBLE.md — The Kingdom (working title: Aldreth)

Companion to `MASTER_PLAN.md`. Covers the kingdom, the magic system, and the mystery the campaign is building toward. **Section 4 is GM-only** — the true shape of the threat, kept separate from what any character currently knows, so future dialogue doesn't accidentally spoil itself.

---

## 1. The Kingdom of Aldreth *(placeholder — rename freely, same spirit as "Vael Academy")*

One crown, but the land is actually held by three Great Houses, each ruling a region for generations, on top of the crown's own central holdings:

- **Emberwing** holds the sun-drenched southern reach.
- **Tidemark** holds the coast — Leviathan banner, literal tides.
- **Thornrook** holds the western forests and wilds — old libraries as likely to be hidden under trees as in a city.

**Vael Academy** sits apart from all three, in **the Vael Reach** — old crown land at the historical heart of the kingdom, where the Warders' stronghold once stood. All three Great Houses send their heirs here, onto ground none of them controls. Kai's village, **Oakstead**, is in the Reach too — he's an outsider to all three houses twice over, by blood and by geography, before anyone's even met him.

## 2. Magic, Bloodline, and the Three Great Houses

Nobody in Aldreth learns magic from nothing. It runs in blood, traced to a handful of old lines collectively called **the Warders** — an order that no longer exists, but whose descendants are, functionally, the nobility. No lineage, no magic. Not "rarely" — essentially never, in living memory, until Kai. *(What the houses don't dwell on: the Warder lines were never the source — they were the **First Crown's** servants, each trained to one facet of a power that was never theirs. See §3.)*

The three houses' leanings are inherited specialties from the three founding Warder lines:

- **Emberwing's** line fought — front-line battle-mages and blade-work.
- **Tidemark's** line held — the literal ward-keepers, defense and faith.
- **Thornrook's** line watched — archivists and theorists.

Everyone at Vael is on a first-name basis — students, not courtiers — except one name per house that's already famous across the kingdom before they ever arrive: each Great House's actual heir (Perrin, Serra, and Corin, for Emberwing, Tidemark, and Thornrook respectively).

## 3. The First Crown — the Ancient Royal Magic *(NEW CANON, story spine v2 — 2026-07)*

> **Direction change.** The old "the wardstones are failing" premise is **demoted**. It is no longer the engine of the story. The heart of the campaign is now **ancient royal magic, a lost bloodline, a relic blade, an academy built over the old source, and a presumed-dead prince who wants the power for himself.** Wardstones survive only as *lesser, later infrastructure* — see below.

Before the three Great Houses, before the Warders ever split into fight / hold / study, there was one line and one source: **the First Crown** — the ancient royal bloodline that first drew magic out of the land, and the power itself, which people now barely have a true name for. All magic in the kingdom descends from it. The three founding Warder lines were its **servants and specialists** — branches trained to wield one facet each (Emberwing fought, Tidemark held, Thornrook watched) on the Crown's behalf. Over generations the source was lost, sealed, or forgotten, the royal line thinned and scattered, and the *branches* — the noble houses — became all that remained. The kingdom's nobles are, without quite knowing it, the caretakers of a power whose original owners are gone.

**Wardstones (demoted).** Ordinary wardstones are real but minor: a **later, lesser attempt** by the Warders to *replicate and channel* First-Crown magic without the royal line to anchor it — standing stones scattered through the old country, mostly dormant, treated as folklore. Oakstead's **Greystone** is one: old rock, kids dare each other to touch it, genuinely harmless. They are a *misunderstanding of the real thing* — which is exactly why the Academy's whole ward-lore is quietly built on a wrong premise.

**What Kai found is not a wardstone.** It is a **First Crown sanctum** — a hidden royal chamber holding a core of the old power, sealed to open only for the blood of the Crown. It answered *him*, specifically, because he carries that blood.

**Reconciling the shipped library lore (it was already seeding this).** The old records call the First Crown line **"the fourth line"** — the royal source alongside the three houses, the one they branched *from*, gone silent in the record. The game's existing library shelves already plant exactly this and need **no rewrite**: `data/dialogue/lore_houses.json` ("Emberwing fought, Tidemark held, Thornrook watched… and the fourth refused nothing"), `castle_lore.json` ("Of the fourth line, the record thereafter is silent"), `beat_first_spark.json` (Kai: "Four founding lines. What if the fourth line didn't end?"), and `lore_wardstones.json` ("What sleeps beneath Vael is not a tool. It is a trust"). Read those four as **First Crown** seeds; the wardstones in them are the houses' imperfect stewardship of the real thing, not a separate failing-wards plot.

## 3b. The Modern Crown and the Prince Who Died *(NEW CANON)*

There is still a king and a court today — the visible remnant of the old royal line, ruling the crown's central holdings while the three houses hold the regions. Most of what the court "knows" about the First Crown is myth and lost record; the true power has been dormant so long it's treated as legend even in the palace.

The king had a **younger brother** — a prince — who *believed* the legend, and spent his life chasing it: the First Crown's true power, the source, the way back to what the royal line used to be. He was declared **dead** years ago (a disappearance, an expedition that never returned — the kingdom mourned and moved on). He is not dead. He has been searching the world ever since. He is closer than anyone, and he is **furious** that the source finally answered — not to him, after all his years, but to *a forager's son from Oakstead who wandered into a cave.* He is the campaign's faced antagonist. (Full bible: `VILLAIN_BIBLE.md`. Seed him as a shadow and a pressure, never a prologue monologue.)

## 4. GM-Only — What's Actually Confirmed vs. Still Open

*(No character knows most of this at the start. Keep it out of anything player-facing until earned.)*

**Confirmed spine (the story is built on these now):**
- Kai carries the **First Crown bloodline** — the original royal line the three houses all branched from. It's why the sanctum opened for him, why he can eventually learn *any* school (he's upstream of all three specialties), and why "he might be noble" resolves to "he is the lost thing every noble house is a branch of."
- The **relic blade** found with him as an infant is a First Crown artifact — a **key / proof / sealed blade**, not an instant superweapon. It responds to his blood (warms, hums, wakes its light) and is bound up with his awakening. It is his sword. *(Art tie: the green Crown-light it channels once awakened is his established green energy blade.)*
- The **presumed-dead prince** wants the First Crown's power and is hunting the source — and now, Kai.
- **Vael Academy is built over a First Crown site** (see `ACADEMY_BIBLE.md`): the old royal seat / sanctum, which the Academy unknowingly-or-half-knowingly guards and studies as "ward lore."

**Still open (spend when the campaign wants it):**
- Exactly *how* Kai's line survived to a foundling in Oakstead — hidden heir, last of a cadet branch, deliberately smuggled away as an infant. (The prince may know more of this than Kai does.)
- Whether the prince is the *only* faced threat or the first of several.
- How much the Academy director actually knows vs. suspects (`ACADEMY_BIBLE.md`).

## 5. Kai's Incident

Full beat-by-beat is in `CHARACTER_BIBLE_KAI.md`. Short version: wandering further than anyone from Oakstead ever has, Kai enters a cave that has always been there but that he's simply never explored — and inside, a sealed door **opens for him and no one else**, because his blood (and/or the relic he unknowingly carries) is what the old magic was waiting for. It doesn't feel like a puzzle solved; it feels like being *recognized*. Beyond it: a First Crown sanctum, and a stone hanging in the air, ringed with runes. He touches it out of pure curiosity. What comes through him is violent enough to be seen from the village. He wakes changed and marked — and in the hours after, his parents finally tell him the truth: this isn't the first time that forest mattered to his family. They found him there too, an abandoned infant, with a sword beside him. They give him the sword.
