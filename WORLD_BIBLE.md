# WORLD_BIBLE.md — The Kingdom (working title: Aldreth)

Companion to `MASTER_PLAN.md`. Covers the kingdom, the magic system, and the mystery the campaign is building toward. **Section 4 is GM-only** — the true shape of the threat, kept separate from what any character currently knows, so future dialogue doesn't accidentally spoil itself.

## 1. The Kingdom of Aldreth *(placeholder — rename freely, same spirit as "Vael Academy")*

One crown, but the land is actually held by three Great Houses, each ruling a region for generations, on top of the crown's own central holdings:

- **Emberwing** holds the sun-drenched southern reach.
- **Tidemark** holds the coast — Leviathan banner, literal tides.
- **Thornrook** holds the western forests and wilds — old libraries as likely to be hidden under trees as in a city.

**Vael Academy** sits apart from all three, in **the Vael Reach** — old crown land at the historical heart of the kingdom, where the Warders' stronghold once stood. All three Great Houses send their heirs here, onto ground none of them controls. Kai's village, **Oakstead**, is in the Reach too — he's an outsider to all three houses twice over, by blood and by geography, before anyone's even met him.

## 2. Magic, Bloodline, and the Three Great Houses

Nobody in Aldreth learns magic from nothing. It runs in blood, traced to a handful of old lines collectively called **the Warders** — an order that no longer exists, but whose descendants are, functionally, the nobility. No lineage, no magic. Not "rarely" — essentially never, in living memory, until Kai.

The three houses' leanings are inherited specialties from the three founding Warder lines:

- **Emberwing's** line fought — front-line battle-mages and blade-work.
- **Tidemark's** line held — the literal ward-keepers, defense and faith.
- **Thornrook's** line watched — archivists and theorists.

Everyone at Vael is on a first-name basis — students, not courtiers — except one name per house that's already famous across the kingdom before they ever arrive: each Great House's actual heir (Perrin, Serra, and Corin, for Emberwing, Tidemark, and Thornrook respectively).

## 3. Wardstones — and the One That Isn't

Ordinary wardstones are real, minor, and scattered through the old country, especially the Reach: standing stones the Warders raised generations ago, mostly dormant, treated as folklore by anyone living near one. Oakstead calls theirs **the Greystone** — old rock, kids dare each other to touch it, nothing's ever happened, and nothing ever will; it's genuinely harmless.

What Kai found is not one of these. Hidden, uncatalogued, reached through a passage that — as far as anyone can tell — has never opened for anyone else. Whether it's the literal source the ordinary wardstones all connect to, or something older still, is exactly the kind of thing Thornrook's oldest records might eventually help answer.

## 4. GM-Only — What's Actually Happening

*(No character knows this at the start of the story. Keep it out of anything player-facing.)*

The wardstones are failing — slowly, unevenly, not all at once. A few directions for what's underneath, genuinely still open:

**A.** The stones seal something ancient and dangerous that the Warders never destroyed, only leashed. The leash is degrading.

**B.** There's no single "thing" sealed. The wardstones were a filter, keeping magic flowing only through Warder blood. The filter is failing, and magic is starting to reach bloodlines it was never meant to. Kai is the first sign the rule itself is breaking, everywhere.

**C.** A blend of both — it's (B), and someone or something is causing the failure on purpose.

**D. — the theory that ties Kai's origin in too:** Kai isn't bloodless. The hidden stone answered him, specifically, because he carries the blood of a fourth line — the one the three Great Houses' lineages all branched *from*, before the Warders ever split into fight / hold / study. It's why the stone opened for him and no one else, and it's the same thread as "he can eventually learn any school of magic" and "he may be noble after all" — one answer instead of three coincidences.

Lean: B as the kingdom-wide mechanism, D as Kai's specific answer, with room to grow B into C once the campaign wants an antagonist with a face. Not confirmed anywhere in-story — genuinely GM-only until you decide it's time to spend it.

## 5. Kai's Incident

Full beat-by-beat is in `CHARACTER_BIBLE_KAI.md`. Short version: wandering further than anyone else from Oakstead ever has, he finds a hidden passage that opens for him and no one else, follows it to a stone floating in the dark, and touches it out of pure curiosity. What comes through him afterward is violent enough to be seen from the village. He wakes up changed, marked, and — in the hours after — learns from his parents that this isn't the first time that stretch of forest has mattered to his family: they found him there too, as an abandoned infant, years before he ever wandered back on his own.
