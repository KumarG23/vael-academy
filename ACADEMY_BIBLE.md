# ACADEMY_BIBLE.md — Vael Academy

Companion to `MASTER_PLAN.md` and `WORLD_BIBLE.md`.

## 1. What It Is

Officially, the kingdom's premier war college — blade, lance, bow, reason, and faith, organized into three houses, training the noble officer corps. **Unofficially, it is built over a First Crown site** (`WORLD_BIBLE.md` §3): the old royal seat / sanctum at the historical heart of the kingdom, the Warders' stronghold raised *on top of* something far older. The Academy unknowingly — and, among a few of the oldest faculty, half-knowingly — **guards and studies the ancient royal magic**, mislabeled for generations as "ward lore." Its deepest foundations, its sealed rooms, and the oath the students swear all trace back to the First Crown, though almost no one alive understands that. Most students and teachers have no idea that second purpose exists.

Students are 15–18 and **entirely noble-born — from the greatest houses down to the most minor, landless, or newly-raised lines, but noble all the same.** There has never been a common-born student at Vael. Kai is the first and only, ever (see §5). They come to learn blade, lance, bow, reason, and faith with one explicit goal: turn them into good, strong lords who benefit the kingdom. Everyone at Vael goes by first names — they're fellow students, not courtiers — except that one name in each house is one everybody already knows before they arrive: the house's actual heir (see `WORLD_BIBLE.md` §2 — Perrin, Serra, and Rina carry that weight for Emberwing, Tidemark, and Thornrook).

## 2. House Assignment Isn't a Choice — Except Once

Students don't pick a house. They're born into one, following the family's Warder lineage the way a surname would. A Tidemark family's children are Tidemark students; nobody asks a twelve-year-old which color they like.

Kai breaks this completely. He has no Warder lineage to be sorted by, so the system that assigns everyone else simply doesn't apply to him — which is exactly why he's the one student in Academy history who actually gets a real choice, and why each house has a genuine, in-character case for wanting him rather than a menu of flavor text. He's the only person at Vael who's ever had to decide who he is instead of being told.

*Story idea, not a commitment: a short trial period sampling each house before Kai chooses would dramatize this well — could be its own early scene or academy-hub flavor ahead of the Mock Battle. Flagging it, not building it, until you want it.*

## 3. The Castle — The Heart of the Game

**Vael is not a menu. It's the main attraction.** A vast castle-school on old crown ground, raised over the Warders' ruined stronghold: old stone under newer halls, towers that predate the school wrapped in classrooms that don't. Grand where it means to impress, and quietly full of mysteries where it doesn't — sealed doors, a library nobody's read to the bottom of, corridors that don't quite match the floors above them. The player explores it room to room between battles, and this is where the game breathes: where the cast becomes people, where the feel-good moments live, and where a huge share of the art budget goes. Painted backdrops per location — the same painterly pipeline the whole game already uses (`MASTER_PLAN.md` §8), just pointed at rooms instead of battlefields.

Design intent — the school should feel *alive*: NPCs where they belong (housemates in their own hall, students crossing the courtyard, staff at their posts), things to do that aren't fighting, and enough hidden corners and lore that wandering is its own reward. Hogwarts' sense of a place with secrets, dialed to something a small team can actually build — think a handful of rich, hand-authored rooms, not an open world.

**Locations (the four that already exist as systems, now rooms + the new ones):**
- **Common Hall** — the great feast hall, the houses' shared social heart. Meals with friends, rotating conversations, support scenes, the noticeboard of daily life.
- **Library** — towering, ancient, under-catalogued. Where the truth about the First Crown hides in plain sight, filed under centuries of "ward lore" that gets the real thing *wrong*. Home to quizzes/study, research beats, and slow-drip mystery reveals. Its sealed **Restricted Stacks** (built) hold the records that don't fit the official story — the thread Rina of Thornrook is already closest to pulling.
- **Training Grounds** — the old muster field. Sparring, weapon practice, the training-XP system, and where combat *lessons* (see teachers, §4) play out.
- **Quartermaster** — armory and stores, once the Warders' own arsenal. Roster, inventory, trading, promotion items.
- **Mission Board / War Room** — where assignments and briefings are posted and the next deployment is chosen. Less corkboard, more "the room where a war college does its actual job."
- **The grounds & waterside** — courtyard, gardens, and a lake or river along the walls: where fishing lives, and open-air scenes.

**Things to do (each is its own small system — build one at a time, not all at once):**
- **Fishing** — at the waterside. Its own mini-game; a relaxing counterweight to combat.
- **Cooking** — shared meals that can grant small buffs or bond points (the meal-with-friends beat, Three Houses-style).
- **Quizzes / study / exams** — in the library and classrooms; end-of-term tests as recurring events (see §4).
- **Parties & holidays** — special authored events on the calendar; pure feel-good, character-forward set pieces.
- **Conversations** — the biggest one. Freely walking up to a character and talking is what a menu can never do, and it's where the cast earns their bibles.

*Build note (not a design change — just honesty about order): the shell comes first — walk the castle, move between rooms, approach and talk to an NPC, trigger an activity. That's a one-time foundation. Every mini-game above is then a bolt-on added on its own schedule. The existing menu-hub systems become the working guts these rooms are painted over.*

## 4. Calendar & Traditions

The academic year is the spine of the campaign — the player lives through one, and its rhythm alternates high-stakes battle with the warm, low-stakes school life that makes the battles matter.

**The year is bracketed by two inter-house battles.** Both are the Mock Battle rite — student against student, non-lethal by long tradition (yield, don't kill your own future officer corps), a deliberate echo of how the founding Warder lines tested each other before the real thing:
- **Opening battle** — early in the year, right after house choice. The house sizes each other up. *(This is the currently-built Chapter 1 content — WO-4.1 — just reframed as the year's opening rite rather than a standalone chapter. No rebuild.)*
- **Closing battle** — the year's climax. Higher stakes, everything the player and their house have grown into, tested against rivals who've grown too.

**Between them, the year fills with life:**
- **Terms & exams** — end-of-term tests (library/classroom events) that reward the study and training the player's been doing; a soft, recurring goalpost.
- **Homework / assignments** — light between-mission tasks; a reason to visit rooms and talk to people.
- **Holidays** — authored calendar events, house rituals and kingdom-wide festivals, pure feel-good.
- **Parties & feasts** — set-piece social scenes; character-forward, no combat, high warmth.
- **Meals with friends** — the recurring small beat (Common Hall / cooking) that quietly builds bonds.
- **Teacher lessons** — each house's teacher (see the character bibles) runs sessions that double as tutorial flavor and character texture.

Underneath all of it, the kingdom's mystery (`WORLD_BIBLE.md` §3–4) escalates quietly — a rumor of the dead prince's agents, another impossible awakening elsewhere, something stirring in the old foundations under the school — so the feel-good year and the gathering threat are always running at once. That contrast is the engine of the whole tone.

## 4b. Campus Visual & Spatial Direction *(Neal, 2026-07-05)*

The campus is for **nobles**: huge, beautiful, slightly gaudy — gold trim, stained glass, banners, marble. Never modest.

**Chrono Trigger is the spatial reference for exploration spaces:**
- **Truce (town)** — alive through multiple elevations, little alleys, fountains, gardens, NPCs walking, buildings connected naturally.
- **Guardia Castle** — the castle blueprint: courtyards, dorm rooms, classrooms, hallways, towers, throne room (ours: the headmaster's), training areas.
- **Zeal** — "almost a magic university already": libraries, magic research, teleporters, crystal architecture. The pretty ceiling to aim at.
- **Enhasa / Kajar / Magic Cave** — magic classrooms, libraries, laboratories, mystical atmosphere.

**Battle maps** (applies to §M-maps too): more color, many terrain types with real bonuses, healing stations, towers, battles *inside* buildings; unique map per chapter. The FE ROM-hack map pack (Neal's Dropbox, mirrored at `assets/reference/map_pack/` if promoted) is the structural baseline — well-composed but *bland*; we go more detailed and extravagant. ## 5. Admission — By Ancient Law

Standard path: noble children, enrolled at the right age, assigned by lineage. Kai arrives by a different door entirely, and it isn't charity or petition — **it's law.** By an old statute nobody's had reason to invoke in living memory, *any* child in whom magic and mana awaken must go to Vael Academy. Full stop. **NEW CANON (spine v2):** the statute isn't really about schooling — it's a **First Crown protocol**, written into the Academy's founding, for the one thing that would matter: magic waking where it *shouldn't*. It was framed as "any child," but in a kingdom where magic only ever meant a noble child it was never a hardship, just dormant protocol — a tripwire nobody expected to trip. Nobody ever imagined it firing for a forager's son from Oakstead. That it fired *at all* is the first sign to those who understand it that something at the old source has stirred.

The mechanism: the local garrison gets wind of "a boy in the town with magic." Word spreads — too strange to stay quiet. And the moment it's confirmed, the ancient law does the rest: a representative of the Academy — dispatched by **the director, who recognizes at once that an awakening like this cannot be ignored** — comes to collect Kai. Not a request. He's leaving, whether Oakstead, his parents, or Kai himself likes it. That compulsion forces the goodbye, and it's the hours before he's collected that his parents finally tell him the truth and give him the relic blade (`CHARACTER_BIBLE_KAI.md` — Origin). *(The director should be firm and knowing, never villainous — someone who understands the stakes and won't say so yet.)*

The tension isn't over whether he's *allowed* in — the law settles that — it's over a commoner walking Vael's halls at all. Plenty at the Academy resent it even though the statute leaves them no say. Good friction to write: the law compels his presence; it can't compel anyone to accept it.
